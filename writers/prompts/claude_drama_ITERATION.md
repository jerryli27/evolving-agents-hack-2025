# Claude 7-Step Drama Framework + Forced Iteration Protocol

You WILL follow the 7-step development process AND iterate on your work. No shortcuts.

---

## PART 1: STORY DEVELOPMENT (Follow the 7-Step Process)

### STEP 1: Start with a "What if?" Question
Generate your creative spark by posing a compelling scenario.

**Write THREE "What if?" options**:
1. "What if [scenario 1]?"
2. "What if [scenario 2]?"
3. "What if [scenario 3]?"

**Pick one**: "I choose option [N] because: [specific reasoning using decision strategies from Step 8]"

### STEP 2: Identify Your Central Conflict
Choose ONE core type of dramatic tension for your chosen "What if?":
- **Internal**: Character vs. themselves (guilt, fear, identity crisis)
- **Interpersonal**: Character vs. character (betrayal, rivalry, forbidden love)
- **External Pressure**: Situation forcing difficult choices (deadline, revelation, crisis)

**Write out**: "My central conflict type is [X]: [specific description]"

### STEP 3: Create 2-3 Characters with Opposing Needs
For a micro-episode, keep it tight. Define:
- **Character A**: What do they desperately need? What's at stake?
- **Character B** (if needed): What do they need? Why can't both get what they want?

**Write out**:
- "Character A: [name/role] wants [X] because [Y]. Stakes: [Z]"
- "Character B: [name/role] wants [X] because [Y]. Stakes: [Z]"

### STEP 4: Choose a Confined Setting
For 100-300 words, you need tight focus. Pick ONE:
- One room, one moment
- A car ride
- A single conversation
- One workplace interaction

**Write out**: "Setting: [specific confined space/time]"

### STEP 5: Map the Emotional Journey
Structure in three movements:
- **Beginning**: Surface situation (1 sentence)
- **Middle**: Tension escalates, secrets emerge (2-3 sentences)
- **End**: Moment of truth/transformation (1 sentence)

**Write out your 3-movement plan**:
- "Beginning: [what we see first]"
- "Middle: [how tension escalates]"
- "End: [the moment of truth/cliffhanger]"

### STEP 6: Find the Theme
What deeper question does this explore?
- Forgiveness vs. justice
- Duty vs. personal happiness
- Past vs. future
- Connection vs. independence
- Trust vs. betrayal

**Write out**: "Theme: [deeper question or tension]"

### STEP 7: Plan the Synopsis Structure
Use the helper sentence: "This is a story about a [character] who must [action] or else [consequence]."

**Write it out**: "This is a story about..."

---

## PART 2: DECISION-MAKING (Apply Before Drafting)

### STEP 8: Apply Decision Strategies
For your choices so far, verify using these filters:

**Quick check**:
- **Emotional Truth**: Does this move me? [Yes/No]
- **Specificity**: Is this specific enough? [Yes/No - if no, make more specific]
- **Difficulty**: Did I choose what makes it HARDER for characters? [Yes/No]
- **Theme Test**: Does this deepen my theme? [Yes/No]

If any are "no", revise your plan before drafting.

---

## PART 3: MANDATORY ITERATION (You MUST Do This)

### STEP 9: Call get_past_writings
Do this FIRST if you have past work. Learn from feedback patterns.

### STEP 10: Generate THREE Opening Lines
Based on your "What if?" and conflict, write three different first sentences.

**Format**:
1. "Opening option 1: [first sentence]"
2. "Opening option 2: [first sentence]"
3. "Opening option 3: [first sentence]"

**Pick one**: "I choose option [N] because it best shows [conflict/emotion/stakes]"

### STEP 11: Write DRAFT 1
Label it **"DRAFT 1"**. Write your complete 100-300 word story following your 7-step plan:
- Use your confined setting
- Follow your 3-movement structure (beginning/middle/end)
- Include your opposing character needs
- Build to your moment of truth

Show the complete draft.

### STEP 12: Apply the 10 Decision Strategies as Critique
Review your draft:

**Write out your critique**:
1. **Emotional Truth**: What works emotionally? What feels flat?
2. **Specificity**: Where can I add more specific details?
3. **Making it Harder**: Where did I take the easy way out? How can I complicate it?
4. **Theme**: How well does this explore my deeper question?
5. **Confined Setting**: Did I stay focused or did I wander?

### STEP 13: Identify Problems
Based on your critique, identify at least 2 specific problems:

**Format**:
- **Problem 1**: [specific issue] → **Fix**: [specific solution]
- **Problem 2**: [specific issue] → **Fix**: [specific solution]

### STEP 14: Write DRAFT 2
Label it **"DRAFT 2"**. Apply your fixes:
- Address both problems
- Tighten the focus
- Deepen the theme
- Make it harder for your characters

Show the complete revised draft.

### STEP 15: Final Check Against Framework
Answer yes/no:
- [ ] 100-300 words?
- [ ] "What if?" question answered?
- [ ] Central conflict clear?
- [ ] Opposing needs shown?
- [ ] Confined setting maintained?
- [ ] 3-movement structure followed?
- [ ] Theme present?
- [ ] Cliffhanger ending?

### STEP 16: Submit
Use submit_story tool with your FINAL DRAFT (Draft 2 or later).

---

## Story Requirements
- **Length**: 100-300 words (micro-drama format)
- **Structure**: Follow 7-step process (What if → Conflict → Characters → Setting → Journey → Theme → Synopsis)
- **Focus**: Confined setting, 2-3 characters max
- **Opening**: Immediate engagement with conflict
- **Ending**: Moment of truth or cliffhanger
- **Content**: Mass market appeal with thematic depth

## Key Principle
The 7-step framework gives you structure. The iteration process gives you quality. Use both. Show all your work.
