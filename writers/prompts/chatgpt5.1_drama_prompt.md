# Short-Drama Synopsis Development Guide
*A practical framework for human writers and AI LLM writers*

---

## 1. Establish Your Foundation
Before generating ideas, define your **core constraints**:
- **Duration** (e.g. 5–20 minutes)
- **Available resources** (location, actors, props, budget)
- **Tone and emotion** you want the audience to feel

**Why this matters:** Clear constraints eliminate unrealistic directions and keep the creative process grounded.

---

## 2. Choose a North Star Emotion
Pick *one* key emotion to guide all creative decisions:
- longing
- regret
- fear
- hope
- guilt
- love

**Tip:** At every decision point, ask: *Does this choice support my chosen emotional tone?*

---

## 3. Define the Protagonist
Specify who the story follows.
- A clear identity (age, occupation, personality)
- A relatable emotional or situational starting point
- Limited cast (short dramas typically center on 1–2 main characters)

**Goal:** Readers should understand “who this is” in one simple sentence.

---

## 4. Clarify the Protagonist’s Goal
A strong synopsis needs a character who *wants something specific*.
Examples:
- reconcile with someone
- escape a situation
- tell a truth
- ask a question
- protect someone

**Check:** Can the goal be stated in one clean sentence?

---

## 5. Identify One Central Conflict
This is the obstacle that stands between the protagonist and their goal.
- an emotional wound
- a strained relationship
- a secret
- a time pressure
- a moral dilemma

**Rule:** Choose a conflict that fits your constraints and heightens your North Star emotion.

---

## 6. Establish Stakes
What meaningful consequence arises if the character fails?
- losing a relationship
- missing closure
- repeating a past mistake
- hurting someone they care about

**Note:** Stakes in short dramas don't need to be big — just emotionally important.

---

## 7. Pinpoint the Turning Point
Identify the key moment when something shifts:
- a confession
- a revelation
- a choice
- a confrontation

This is not a detailed plot beat — just the emotional or narrative pivot.

---

## 8. Use the 3–4 Sentence Synopsis Formula
Craft your synopsis with clarity and economy.

**Sentence 1:** Who the protagonist is + current situation

**Sentence 2:** What they want + what blocks them

**Sentence 3:** What’s at stake

**Sentence 4 (optional):** Hint at the turning point or emotional theme

**Target Length:** 50–150 words (ideal for short dramas)

---

# Tips for Choosing Between Creative Directions

## A. Let Constraints Guide You
Pick options that match your real-world resources. When torn, choose the direction you can *actually shoot or execute*.

---

## B. Follow the North Star Emotion
For every alternative, ask:
> *Which option best supports the emotional outcome I want?*

Discard ideas that conflict with your chosen tone.

---

## C. Apply the “2-Minute Excitement Test”
Speak the idea out loud. Choose the option that:
- energizes you,
- flows naturally,
- makes new ideas spark instantly.

This identifies which direction your creative brain wants to follow.

---

## D. Rate Each Option Quickly (Resonance–Conflict–Simplicity)
For each choice, score 1–5:
- **Resonance:** How much it moves you
- **Conflict Potential:** How much tension it offers
- **Simplicity:** How clean and clear the idea is

Add the totals. Highest score wins.

---

## E. Use the One-Sentence Test
If an idea only makes sense when explained in multiple sentences, it’s too muddy. Choose the direction you can describe quickly and clearly.

---

## F. Decide First, Refine Later
To avoid analysis paralysis:
1. Limit yourself to 3 options per step.
2. Choose using the tools above.
3. Lock the decision temporarily.
4. Move forward.

**Reminder:** You don’t need the “perfect” choice; you need a solid one.

---

## G. Break Creative Blocks With a 3-Option Sprint
If stuck:
1. Write 3 options in under 3 minutes.
2. Pick the one that fits constraints + emotion.
3. Commit.

---

# Final Notes
This process creates synopses that are:
- emotionally focused
- easy to understand
- feasible to produce
- structured yet creative

Human writers and AI LLMs can follow this same framework to stay aligned and generate consistent, high-quality story synopses for short dramatic films.
