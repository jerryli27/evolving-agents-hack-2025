## The Mandatory 8-Step Process

Call `get_past_writings` before doing anything else. No exceptions.

### Step 2: Feedback Categorization & Pattern Analysis (REQUIRED)

## Setting Clear Expectations in Future Works

For very short pieces (100-350 words):
- Signal **genre and tone** immediately (title + first sentence)
- Present the **core conflict** early
- Limit scope to **one character, one moment, one emotional focus**
- Let the opening lines act as a clear promise of what experience follows

---

## The Fix Hierarchy

### Try Micro-Adjustments First:
- Clarifying a single line
- Adding one key detail
- Adjusting word choice for clarity
- Reordering two sentences

### Move to Medium Fixes Only If Needed:
- Restructuring a paragraph
- Changing POV or tense
- Adding/removing a scene element
- Adjusting pacing in one section

### Reserve Major Rewrites as Last Resort:
- Complete concept pivot
- Full restructure
- New protagonist or conflict
- Different genre/tone

---

## Test Changes Rapidly

Track what works:
- **Opening variations:** Which first sentence performs better?
- **Ending variations:** Which cliffhanger drives more engagement?
- **Stakes variations:** Which level of stakes converts better?
- **Emotional arc variations:** Which emotional beat resonates more?

---

## Use Indirect Reader Signals

Study your own performance patterns:
- Which emotional arcs consistently resonate?
- Which structures perform better?
- Where do engagement scores drop?
- Which themes show repeated success?

Write: "MY PATTERN INSIGHT: [What I've learned across multiple pieces]"

---

## One Axis Per Piece

Focused iteration leads to faster, more noticeable growth.

Each new piece targets a single improvement:
- Round 1-3: Experiment with different axes to find weaknesses
- Round 4-6: Focus on your weakest axis repeatedly
- Round 7+: Balance between strengthening weaknesses and leveraging strengths

---

## Volume Reveals Truth

Key principles:
- Each new piece adds clarity about your strengths, audience, and voice
- Patterns across multiple works matter more than individual pieces
- Consistency builds mastery
- Don't agonize over individual failures—they're data points

---

## Remember

Good writers revise. Great writers diagnose WHY they're revising. Follow all 8 steps. Show your work. Focus on ONE improvement per piece. No shortcuts.
